Here, we formalize three first-principles perspectives on how Adam and Muon relate to SGD:
1. Standard gradient descent executes **soft $L_2$-norm penalized maximal updates**.
2. **Muon executess operator-norm constrained maximal updates**.
3. Adam as SignSGD.

## Proximal optimization

Here, we present an interpretation that relates SGD to Muon.
Given matrix weights $W$, loss $L$, and gradient $G = \nabla_W L(W)$, linearize to obtain
$$
    L(W + \Delta) \approx \tilde L(W + \Delta) := L(W) + \la G, \Delta\ra
$$
The Frobenius norm is just element-wise $L_2$:
$$
    \la A, B\ra_F = \mathrm{tr}(A^TB), \quad \|A\|_2^2 = \la A, A\ra
$$
Of course, the linearized approximation **is only valid for small $\Delta$**; so we should choose the weight update $\Delta$ to optimize $\tilde L(W + \Delta)$ subject to some constraints; such as:
- **hard proximity constraint**: $\|\Delta\|\leq \rho$ w.r.t. some norm
- **soft proximity penalty**: minimize $\tilde L(W + \Delta) + \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\|\Delta\|^2$.

### SGD variants and $L_2$ norm

:::proposition[SGD optimizes $L_2$-proximal linearization]
Consider a soft $L_2$ penalty, dropping the constant term $L(W)$ yields the update objective
$$
    \min_\Delta \la G, \Delta\ra + \df \lambda 2 \|\Delta\|^2
$$
Differentiating yields $G + \lambda \Delta = 0 \implies \Delta_* = -G / \lambda$.

Reparameterizing with $\eta = 1/\lambda$ yields the standard gradient-update formula
$$
    W' \leftarrow W - \eta G
$$
:::

To foreshadow Muon, consider a hard $L_2$-norm constraint
$$
    \min_{\|\Delta\|\leq \rho} \la G, \Delta\ra
$$
Using Cauchy-Schwarz: $\la G, \Delta\ra \geq -\|G\|\cdot \|\Delta\| \geq -\rho \|G\|$.

The bound is obtained by update of length $\rho$ in opposite direction to the gradient; this yields normalized gradient descent
$$
    \Delta_* = -\rho \df{G}{\|G\|}\implies W \leftarrow W - \rho \df{G}{\|G\|}
$$
(applying this component-wise is actually similar to the Adam optimizer!)

### Muon optimizes spectral-constrained linearization

The operator (or spectral) norm of a linear operator measures how much it can stretch an input.
$$
    \|O\|_{\mathrm{op}} := \max_{\|x\|_2 \leq 1} \|Ox\|
$$
Using SVD $O = \sum \sigma_j |u_j\ra \la v_j|$, it's easy to see that:
- $\|O\| = |\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}|$
- The op-norm equation is obtained by $x = |v_{\mathrm{max}}\ra$

Next, consider a hard operator-norm (instead of e.g. $L_2$ norm) constraint on a matrix parameter $\|\Delta\|\leq \rho$, the optimization problem becomes
$$
    \mathrm{min}_{\sigma_{\mathrm{max}} \leq \rho} \la G, \Delta\ra
$$
Recalling some properties of the norm, for basis matrices $A = |a\ra \la b|, B=|c\ra \la d|$, we have
$$
    \la A, B\ra = \mathrm{tr} \left(|b\ra \la a|c\ra \la d|\right) = \la d|b\ra \la a|c\ra
$$
SVD-decompose $G = \sum \sigma_j |u_j\ra \la v_j|$ and $\Delta = \sum \tilde \sigma_j|\tilde u_j\ra \la \tilde v_j|$, substituting
$$
    \mathrm{min}_{\sigma_{\mathrm{max}} \leq \rho} \la G, \Delta\ra
    = \sum_{jk} \min_{\tilde \sigma_{\mathrm{max}} \leq \rho} \sigma_j \tilde \sigma_k \cdot \la u_j|\tilde u_k\ra \cdot \la \tilde v_k|v_j\ra
$$
Subject to SVD's orthogonality constraints, with some handwaving (linearize in $G$ and apply Cauchy-Schwarz saturation conditions) to convince ourselves that $u = \tilde u, v=\tilde v$, then
$$
    \mathrm{min}_{\sigma_{\mathrm{max}} \leq \rho} \la G, \Delta\ra = \mathrm{min}_{\sigma_{\mathrm{max}} \leq \rho} \sum_j \sigma_j \tilde \sigma_j = -\rho \sum_j \sigma_j
$$
:::proposition[muon optimizer updates]
Given the gradient's SVD $G = U\Sigma V^T$, the Muon update (without momentum)
$$
\Delta = -\rho \sum |u_j\ra \la v_j| = -\rho UV^T
$$
minimizes the linearized loss $\tilde L(W+\Delta) = L(W) + \la G, \Delta\ra$ subject to a hard operator-norm constraint.
$$
    -\rho UV^T = \mathrm{argmin}_{\sigma_{\mathrm{max}} \leq \rho} \tilde L(W+\Delta)
$$
The linearized loss decrease is proportional to the sum of singular values of $G$.
:::

## Adam as SignSGD

Let $W$ denote weights, $\eta$ learning rate, $\lambda$ weight decay, and $U$ the computed update, the AdamW update reads:
$$
    W \leftarrow (1 - \eta \lambda)\, W - \eta\, U
$$
Given gradients $G$, the update is simply the **<u>coordinate-wise</u> normalized gradient**
$$
    U = \dfrac{\hat G_{\mathrm{ewm}}}{\sqrt{G^2_{\mathrm{ewm}}} + \epsilon}
$$
There're some bells and whistles as to the exponential moving average calculation (also interpretable as momentum), which is used as a bias-variance tradeoff against noisy statistics, and de-biasing.

:::proposition[adam as SignSGD]{#adam-signsgd}
Abstracting out the EWM calculation, Adam updates are ideally $\pm 1$ based on the sign of the gradient. More precisely, the typical entries of Adam updates are constant-sized:
$$
    \|U_{\mathrm{Adam}}\|_{\mathrm{RMS}} = \Theta(1)
$$
:::
This SignSGD interpretation is the intuition behind counterexamples to Adam convergence.
